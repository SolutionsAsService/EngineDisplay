How the models are made

Nothing here is a downloaded mesh. One Python script, cad/build_engines.py, builds every part as a solid in FreeCAD 1.1.3 from a handful of numbers per engine: cylinder count, bore, stroke, rod length, cylinder pitch and crank throw angles. Change a number, run it and then tools/build_pages.py, and the block, crank, pistons, rods and the site's tables all follow.

From solids to the page

  1. FreeCAD builds each part with its own origin: pistons at the gudgeon pin, rods at the small end, the crank on its axis.
  2. Each face is meshed separately, so edges stay crisp, and written to a GLB with one node per part and the crank geometry (radius, rod length, cylinder axes, throw angles) stored in the file.
  3. In the browser, the pistons and rods are placed every frame by slider-crank geometry, so the animation is the mechanism, not a recorded clip.
  4. The same parts are exported as STEP (for any CAD program, offered below) and as STL, which is not offered for download (10 to 55 MB) but is rebuilt by the script; the STL was inspected with the Unified CAD MCP's cad_inspect_stl_geometry: 0 inverted shells on all three; the full integrity check did not run because the meshes exceed the tool's triangle limit.

What is simplified

These are teaching models, not engineering drawings. Valves, the oil pump, gaskets and fasteners (apart from the connecting-rod cap bolts) are left out. The radial's link rods are drawn as plain rods all riding one crankpin, rather than hanging off a master rod. Proportions follow the bore, stroke and rod length; detail parts are sized by eye.

Models are generic, illustrative engines built as parametric solids in FreeCAD 1.1.3 by cad/build_engines.py; they are not any manufacturer's design. Source and STEP files: github.com/SolutionsAsService/EngineDisplay. MIT licence.
